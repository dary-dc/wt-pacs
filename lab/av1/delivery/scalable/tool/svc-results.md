this relay, not an unlimited link; their cells have n = 0–10, and nothing is claimed for `none` beyond the numbers in
them.

## The rule

Part 5's rule, applied as written to the cells of its own that ran (20 Mbit/s at 1× and 4×; `none` is the metrics
protocol's link, not the rule's): see the last table. **Not decided by this sitting.** The rule needs t_P at 5 Mbit/s
and every one of its cells (5 and 50 Mbit/s, loss, jitter), and those have not run. On the cells that ran, **every
clause it can decide passes for `a2-q20`, `a2-q40`, `p2-q20` and `p2-q40` on all three volumes**: frames exact, t_P
0.009–0.018 of HTJ2K's, more slices pictured at 15 slices/s than prefix-first, t_X 0.988–1.012× and bytes 0.989–1.007×
the shipped payload's. **`p3-m10` fails the bytes clause on all three volumes** (1.040–1.170) and t_X on `dbts_b1`
(1.170–1.172, beyond the A/A resolution of ±0.1 %), so under the rule it cannot pass on two. `p3-m20` and `p3-m30` fail
the bytes clause on `dbts_b1` only (1.044, 1.022) and pass every decided clause on `dbts_a1` and `dbts_c1`. Part 6's
verdict stands, as 10 says.
