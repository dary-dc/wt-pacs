# Results of the investigations, in detail (RESULTS-BRIEF, 2026-11-22)

What the investigations found, for the owner: by area, most important first. Each result has five parts:

* one sentence in plain words;
* the mechanism;
* the numbers, each with its label;
* how it was found;
* its limits, and the file and commit it came from.

Then two closing sections: what was refuted or corrected, and what is not yet known.

This brief runs nothing and decides nothing. It was written by a session that ran none of the rows. It reads:

* the decision pages on `claude/reviews-2026-11-28` (`docs/decisions/`);
* the line docs at their newest copy (`claude/line-docs-3`, and `claude/names-unified` for `coordination.md` and
  `proposals.md`);
* the Result sections of the status files on `claude/work-ledger` (`docs/queue/*.md`) for rows started since
  2026-11-01;
* the evidence files those name.

**Labels.** A number is labelled the way its source labels it:

* *measured*: run and recorded;
* *derived*: computed from measured numbers;
* *theory*: predicted by a model;
* *source*: quoted from a document outside the project;
* *unlabelled*: the source gives it no label.

Where two sources disagree, both are given.

**One bench under almost everything.** Unless an item says otherwise, the bench is the same, and every result
inherits its limits:

* one 4-core cloud host without a GPU;
* headless Chromium with software decoding;
* a userspace relay or loopback in place of a real link;
* the three EA1141 DBT volumes `dbts_a1`, `dbts_b1` and `dbts_c1`, or all 19 EA1141 volumes for byte counts.

So nothing here was measured on a GPU, on Safari, on a radio link or on a breast ultrasound cine. One phone (Android 16,
Chrome 157) ran one evening on one home link, and its numbers are the only phone numbers there are.

**Sources.** A source written `branch:path @ sha` names the commit that wrote the evidence. Rows are
`claude/work-ledger:docs/queue/<ROW>.md`.

## 1. Transport and delivery

### 1.1 WebTransport stays the only wire; no GET path for now

**Plain.** fetch over HTTP/2 or HTTP/3, and WebSocket, each beat WebTransport in a few conditions and tie it in most.
The recommendation is to keep WebTransport as the one wire, and first to test whether WebTransport's own existing
flags win the same cases.

* **Mechanism.** All four stacks run Cubic under the same 6 MiB stream credit. The controller sets the fill rate, not
  the framing. They differ in four places:
  * an ask that waits behind queued fill;
  * idle time, which costs TCP its window;
  * one stream's credit, which binds on a very long delay;
  * CPU spent in userspace QUIC.
* **Numbers** (measured; each is a ratio to WebTransport, WT-TS, as a median):
  * **Clean and public links:** every arm ties, ×1.000–1.003. WT-TS fills in 9.92 s and 10.03 s.
  * **~0.8 s RTT (CR):**
    * HTTP/3 with one GET a frame (H3-frame) ×0.630.
    * WebSocket ×1.088, H2-frame ×1.091, H2-fill ×1.052.
    * WT-TS 35.0 s.
    * Mechanism (theory): one stream's 6 MiB credit over 0.8 s caps that stream at ~63 Mbit/s.
  * **1 % bursty loss:** WebSocket ×0.962, H2-frame ×0.951.
  * **Head-of-line p99:** WebSocket ×0.871, H3-frame ×1.598.
  * **10 ms reorder (ENT):** WebSocket ×0.842, H2 ×0.847–0.858.
  * **Server CPU:** WebSocket and H2 ×0.145–0.162; H3 ×1.075–1.112.
  * **Client CPU:** WebSocket ×0.695, H2 ×0.723–0.770, H3 ×1.628–1.665.
  * **Bytes:** WT-TS carries 1.037× the frames' bytes; the other arms are 1.000–1.002 of WT-TS.
  * **Coverage:** 1 706 counted visits, every frame exact by XXH3.
* **How found.**
  * Rows TRANSPORT-RUN, TRANSPORT-VERDICT-REVIEW and TRANSPORT-AD-RERUN (2026-11-18/19).
  * Eight arms: WT-TS, its A/A copy WT-TS′, WT-WASM, WS, H2-frame, H2-fill, H3-frame and H3-fill.
  * Nine of ten cells.
  * `dbts_b1` as HTJ2K: 50 frames, 2.15 MB a frame.
  * Chromium 157 through a TUN relay capped at 90 Mbit/s.
* **Limits.**
  * Every link profile is an assumed, scaled relay model; none is a radio.
  * The host never saturated (busiest core median 0.08–0.52), so nothing is claimed above 90 Mbit/s.
  * The pooled-connection cell (FP) was not run: the 12-hour stop came first.
  * ENT was cut inside a period, so it is unbalanced, and it filled ×18 slower than predicted. Both are properties of
    the jitter model.
  * Chromium only.
* **Source.**
  * `claude/reviews-2026-11-28:docs/decisions/transport-stack.md @ 20b7f1619`
  * `claude/transport-design:docs/transport/evidence/stacks.md` §6.1–§6.6 `@ dd7548948`
  * rows on `claude/transport-arms:lab/transport-arms/rows/`

### 1.2 A jump during a fill is answered in about half the time by the fill-shape fetch and by WebSocket

**Plain.** When the reader asks for one frame while the series is still downloading, two arms answer in about half
WebTransport's time: the fetch arms that cancel their download, and WebSocket. WebTransport has a "one queue" flag
that may do the same, but it has never been run on this rig. This is the open structural question.

* **Mechanism** (theory, not traced). The fill-shape fetch aborts its range, so the asked frame's GET meets an empty
  send queue. WT-TS's ask shares the connection with up to 6 MiB of fill in flight.
* **Numbers** (measured, ratios to WT-TS; WT-TS answers in 743 ms on the public-link form and 5 287 ms on the LTE
  form):

  | arm | public link | LTE form |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | H3-fill | ×0.548 | ×0.525 |
  | H2-fill | ×0.682 | ×0.501 |
  | WebSocket | ×0.738 | ×0.468 |
  | H3-frame | ×1.809 | ×1.678 |
  | H2-frame | ×1.974 | ×1.932 |

  * The fetch arms had been predicted at ×1.0–2.0 (theory). The fill shapes came in below that band, a miss.
  * WebTransport's one-queue flag measured ×0.32–0.90 on earlier rigs, never this one (`ask-during-a-fill.md`).
  * PR #39 (asks first under a 6 MiB send window) is given on the page, unlabelled: ask ×0.18–0.31, fill +2 to +44 %.
* **How found.**
  * Cell AD re-run: 8 arms, 16 rounds, 256 visits, every frame XXH3-exact. The asked frame landed first in all 64
    fill-shape visits.
  * The first run was void (see §9).
* **Limits.**
  * `dbts_b1` only.
  * The LTE form's ranges are wide (H3-fill 0.387–2.726), because 0.1 % loss spreads every arm.
  * As in 1.1.
* **Owner's call.**
  * Queue one cloud run of WT-TS with `--send-window-follows-cwnd` and with `per-frame` streams, against H3-fill and
    H3-frame. A session that does not run it pre-registers it.
  * PRs #39 and #96 wait for that run.
  * If WT-TS with its flags stays above ×0.8 of the fill shape's ask, a GET path for asks alone becomes the next
    structural draft, to be measured on a phone.
* **Source.** `transport-stack.md` §2 item 2 `@ 20b7f1619`; `stacks.md` §6.6 `@ dd7548948`; the fetch fix
  `claude/transport-arms:lab/transport-arms/fetch-session.ts @ c2802b0c`.

### 1.3 After a 5-second pause the TCP stacks lose their window; WebTransport does not

**Plain.** After the reader pauses for 5 seconds, the next ask over HTTP/2 or WebSocket is 35–70 % slower. WebTransport
and HTTP/3 are not slowed. After a 1-second pause, every stack ties.

* **Mechanism.**
  * Linux's `tcp_cwnd_restart()` halves a TCP window once for each RTO of idle, down to the initial window (source,
    kernel v6.12 and v6.18).
  * quinn 0.11.18 keeps its window through idle.
  * After 1 s, about a quarter of a ~1 600-segment window is left, which is enough for a 1 485-segment frame
    (derived).
* **Numbers** (measured; WT-TS answers in 229–252 ms):
  * At 5 s, public link: WebSocket ×1.391, H2-frame ×1.366, H2-fill ×1.350.
  * At 5 s, 38 ms link: WebSocket ×1.635, H2 ×1.687–1.699.
  * At 5 s, H3: ×1.006–1.021.
  * At 1 s, the TCP arms: ×0.975–1.010.
  * The RTO read was 406–454 ms.
* **How found.** Cell AA: 6 forms, 128 visits a gap. TRANSPORT-VERDICT-REVIEW reproduced it.
* **Limits.**
  * The number of halvings per visit was not traced.
  * The page carries this result to any Linux server and Chromium client (derived).
* **Source.** `stacks.md` §6.1–§6.2 `@ dd7548948`.

### 1.4 `bbr` is not withdrawn after the phone's two failures; it stays an option, never the default

**Plain.** The phone's two Wi-Fi failures both happened under `bbr`, but neither was caused by the server's congestion
controller. So `bbr` stays available and off. Under random loss in the lab, `bbr` is far faster. On the one real phone
run it was not better, and on Wi-Fi `cubic-restart` was faster.

* **Mechanism** (derived). The server's controller paces only what the server sends. It cannot stop the phone's
  packets from reaching the server, and it cannot stall the phone's decoder.
* **Numbers, lab** (measured, medians of 5):
  * At 1 % random loss, `bbr` fills in ×0.11–0.12 of Cubic's time. On `dbts_b1` that is 94 716 ms against 10 024 ms.
  * A jump 500 ms into the fill: ×0.08–0.09 under `bbr`.
  * Clean link: fill ×0.84–0.99; Lossless ×0.89–0.91.
  * Bursty loss: fill ×0.64–0.89.
* **Numbers, phone** (measured):
  * Wi-Fi: `cubic-restart` faster in 9 of 10 pairs, median 1.084 (0.939–1.364).
  * Tether: no difference (n = 11, median 1.049).
  * Load 6: for 30 s no packet from the phone reached either server.
  * Load 13: an 18 s decode call on a page that the run's notes call hidden (see 3.6).
* **How found.**
  * Rows MEASURE-1 (P1, 90 runs) and MEASURE-2 (R2) in the lab.
  * Rows PHONE-RUN and PHONE-BLIND-READ, `dbts_b1-q8-g8`.
* **Limits.**
  * One failure of each kind.
  * Load 13's "hidden" is a session note; visibility was not measured.
  * Why the phone's UDP went silent is not shown.
  * The red team (REDTEAM-PROPOSALS) put `dbts_a1`'s 1 % Lossless gain at ×0.37–0.55, not ×0.29: 5 runs were too few,
    and the comparison should be against P4's identical default arm.
  * By the blind read's 10-minute rule the phone run stops after octet 4, so its fill comparisons are undecided.
* **Owner's call.** The page reads the 2026-11-11 amendment's rule as "not withdrawn". The owner may read it
  otherwise.
* **Source.**
  * `transport-stack.md` §2 item 7 `@ 20b7f1619`
  * `claude/proposals:docs/lines/proposals.md @ 4ab3ee0d` (P1), `8527047e` (red team) and `b6e4b47d` (R2)
  * `claude/phone-rows:docs/phone-rows/README.md @ 4e492c92`

### 1.5 On the one phone, the fill is limited by decoding, not by the network

**Plain.** On the one phone tested, a series took as long as it did to load because the phone decodes slowly. The
network had finished within the first second. A faster link cannot help such a phone much.

* **Mechanism.** Three software AV1 decoders take about 135 ms a frame, against 0.5–1.0 s of wire time for the whole
  series.
* **Numbers** (measured unless marked):
  * Decode is 0.76–0.94 of the fill (median ~0.90).
  * 133–135 ms a frame on every link and controller.
  * The rest of the fill (dial, first frame, idle decoders, last paint) is 0.22–0.35 s.
  * ~21 frames/s with three decoders (derived).
  * Loads 6 and 10 were slow decodes, at 524 and 872 ms a frame, not slow wire (see §9).
* **How found.** Row FILL-SPLIT. The phone run's beacon and server rows: 11–12 loads for each link and controller
  pair (`cubic-restart` and `bbr` over home Wi-Fi and a tether), on `dbts_b1-q8-g8` (50 frames) in Chrome 157.
* **Limits.**
  * One phone, one access point, one evening.
  * `prefer-software` is only a hint, and the decoder Chrome actually used is not recorded.
  * The number of decoders was not varied.
* **Source.** `claude/fill-split:docs/phone-rows/README.md` and `lab/phone/fill.py @ 28810f62`.

### 1.6 The real home Wi-Fi link is nothing like the lab's "home Wi-Fi" model

**Plain.** The measured home link is far faster and cleaner than the lab profile named after it. So lab results under
that profile, including `bbr`'s ×2.12, do not describe this link.

* **Mechanism.** This link loses packets only when a queue overflows (congestion). It shows no random loss.
* **Numbers.**
  * Wi-Fi (measured):
    * Base RTT ~2 ms.
    * Bottleneck at least ~600 Mbit/s.
    * Tail-drop at 22, 40 and 96 ms of queue (p10, p50, p90).
    * 89 of 89 losses were congestive.
  * Tether (measured):
    * ~0.7 ms RTT, ~265 Mbit/s.
    * Losses at 14–26 ms of queue, under Cubic only.
  * The lab's "home Wi-Fi" profile (unlabelled): 30 ms, 15–40 Mbit/s, 0.5 % Gilbert–Elliott loss, 300 ms queue.
  * A relay form that fits the link (derived): `--delay-ms 1 --rate-kbit 600000 --queue-ms 40`, with no loss.
* **How found.** Row LINK-CALIBRATION, from the phone run's server rows.
* **Limits.**
  * One phone, one access point, one evening.
  * The relay was checked only to 100 Mbit/s and has a 0.4 ms floor, so it cannot reproduce this link.
  * Not measured: jitter, uplink, cellular, and any hospital Wi-Fi (no source exists for hospital Wi-Fi).
* **Source.** `claude/link-calibration:docs/transport/transport-conclusions.md` §1 `@ 44cf80c6`.

### 1.7 Which link limits what, from published link profiles

**Plain.** On ordinary home Wi-Fi and 5G, the view is limited by the phone's decoding. On LTE or a weak link, it is
limited by the network. The exact frame on request is limited by round trips.

* **Numbers.**
  * The view needs ~464 KB a frame (source fixture).
  * At ~45 ms effective decode, the decoder is the bottleneck above ~83 Mbit/s of goodput (derived).
  * The exact frame, asked after a silence, needs 5–8 round trips of slow start (0.04–0.53 s), plus 6 MiB ÷ goodput
    when a fill is ahead of it (derived).
  * Cubic's loss bound at 0.01 % random loss and 60 ms is ~20 Mbit/s for one session (theory, RFC 9438).
* **How found.** Row LINK-TARGETS: six published profiles, quoted verbatim, and one fitted link.
* **Limits.**
  * Theory and sources only; nothing was run.
  * One phone's decode.
  * The ITU-R M.2410 figures are targets, not measurements.
* **Source.** `claude/link-targets:docs/transport/link-profiles.md @ a3b35708`.

### 1.8 A lost exact ask is re-asked once, after 1 s (PR 378, re-scoped)

**Plain.** When a request for the exact frame is lost in transit, the viewer now asks once more, a second later,
instead of wrongly showing "Lossless check failed". A refusal or a failed check is never re-asked. The sources
disagree on how long a silence this one re-ask can ride out.

* **Mechanism.**
  * The downloader marks a transport loss as `lost`, and the consumer names it `AskLostError`.
  * The controller re-asks only that error, once, after 1 s, while the reader stays on the frame.
  * Since FIX-378-DIAL, an ask whose own re-dial fails also counts as lost.
* **Numbers.**
  * The original 1-4-16 s schedule, at 7 % loss plus a 45 s blackout (measured, simulated, 3 rounds per arm):
    * off: 3 of 3 failed, at 28.2 s;
    * on: 3 of 3 reached *Lossless*, at 58.1–58.7 s.
  * Mutants: 11 of 11 killed; the review killed 11 of 12, with 1 equivalent.
  * FIX-378-DIAL: the review's test `ee3cadeb` killed the one mutant the author's tests left alive.
* **The two sources disagree.**
  * The integrity page (`frame-integrity.md` §3.6) derives a ~57 s ride-out (28 + 1 + 28).
  * The review of the once-only scope (`lossless-under-loss.md`) says, as theory not re-run, that the single re-ask at
    29.2 s "finds no session and dials inside the outage". The 45 s blackout would then end on *Lossless check failed*
    at ~34 s.
  * The only measured recovery is under the older schedule.
* **How found.** Rows LOSSLESS-UNDER-LOSS, PHONE-FIX-REVIEW, PHONE-FIX-378, FIX-378-DIAL and their reviews.
  `lab/lossless-under-loss` with `link_impair.py`, headless Chromium 153.
* **Limits.**
  * Simulated loss, not a radio.
  * The rig was not re-run under the once-only scope.
  * No source gives a distribution of outages in seconds on a real wireless link. Published LTE handovers are 24–40 ms
    (source).
* **Owner's call.** Merge PR 378, and decide whether one re-ask is enough.
* **Source.**
  * `claude/lossless-under-loss:docs/reviews/lossless-under-loss.md @ ffc407de` (measured), `d9ed9815` (once-only)
    and `60635db78` (FIX-378-DIAL review)
  * `frame-integrity.md` §3.6 `@ 070e19ee8`

### 1.9 A wake on the exact session before *Lossless* is pressed

**Plain.** Nudging the network when the finger touches the Lossless button removes the radio's wake-up delay. It
worked on a modelled radio, but no phone has tested it.

* **Mechanism.** On `pointerdown`, an `end_stream` on the exact session promotes an idle radio before the `click`
  sends the ask. The gain is max(0, P − L).
* **Numbers.**
  * −103 ms on `dbts_a1-q8-g8-cut` and −105 ms on `dbts_c1-q8-g16-cut` (measured; medians of 7, tolerance ±15 ms).
  * Expected on a phone: 0–100 ms (theory, corrected down from 0–150 ms by the red team).
  * Carriers' promotion delays, by report: 190–396 ms on 4G and 341–1 907 ms on 5G.
* **How found.** Rows MEASURE-1 (P3) and MEASURE-2 (R3). A relay with `--idle-promote 5:300`.
* **Limits.**
  * A modelled radio only; nothing is built on the product page.
  * `dbts_b1` was left out, because the browser's 15 s ping wakes the radio first (see §9).
* **What decides it.** The owner's phone on cellular: 20 dwells an arm, adopted at ≥ 30 ms. A keep-alive at ≤ 5 s is
  the owner's call, because it costs energy.
* **Source.** `claude/docs-client:docs/client/evidence/wake-before-lossless.md @ bfa55e4db`; `client-calls.md`
  `@ 819a63ecc`.

### 1.10 Server network defaults

**Plain.** Of the server settings held from PR 254, the page closes two as no, holds two for the next cloud run and
holds one for a test at the real round-trip time. Host networking stays a proposal.

* **Initial window.**
  * It stays quinn's 12 000 B; 32 packets stays opt-in, and PR #37 is closed.
  * 32 packets (38 400 B) helps only a fresh session's first ask (measured):
    * −28 to −33 % when the session is idle;
    * −16 to −33 % behind any queue of 20 packets or more;
    * +11.8 % in one cell.
  * The same window on the exact server made Lossless slower (measured):
    * P4: ×1.06 at 1 % loss;
    * R4 under `bbr`: ×1.09–1.26 in every cell.
* **Held settings.**

  * #39 and #96 wait for 1.2's run.
  * #41 (a 100 ms initial RTT, with the repeated-Initial reply removed) waits for a run at the target's real RTT.
* **Host networking.**
  * Rootless publishing forwards each datagram through single-threaded pasta, without GSO, into a 500-frame tap ring

  * Host mode measured 22.0 datagrams a `sendmsg` under `cubic-restart` and 13.4 under `bbr`.
  * Rootless costs ~16 600 tap reads a fill, against 750–1 200 host-stack walks in host mode (derived).
  * Cubic's window had a median of 10.2 MB and a maximum of 23.5 MB (measured), against the 726 kB ring.
  * Nothing was measured in a forwarded mode, and the check needs a client on a second machine.

## 2. Codecs, quality and the view's claim

### 2.1 The view should claim nothing about what the eye can see (option A)

**Plain.** No measurement or study here can back the words "visually lossless". The recommendation is that the view's
label state the facts (lossy AV1, about N : 1), that the exact frame be the only picture with a quality promise, and
that Q 8 be kept as an interim picture.

* **Mechanism.**
  * In the US, a lossy mammogram "must not be reviewed for primary image interpretations" (source, FDA 2022 §VIII).
  * The ESR warns that visually lossless need not mean diagnostically lossless (source).
  * What every source leaves the product free to say is a description, plus the fact that the original is
    available.
  * Option A needs no fitted constant, so it carries to any device and modality by construction (theory).
* **Numbers.**
  * The Q 8 view is 0.07–0.24 of HTJ2K's bytes over the 19 volumes, and 0.07–0.22 on the three timed volumes
    (measured).
  * With the whole exact series behind it, the total is 1.07–1.24× (derived).
  * One exact frame weighs as much as 4.2–14 view frames. A reader who dwells on a tenth of the slices fetches
    1.4–2.4× the view (derived).
  * The label's N at Q 8 is 20.7–37.5 : 1 on 12-bit volumes and 11.9–29.4 : 1 on 10-bit ones (measured, R9).
* **How found.** Rows VL-CRITERION, its review, fix and re-review (no blocker), then VL-READS (PR 420) and VL-PAGE.
* **Limits.**
  * 19 DBT volumes of one dataset: 12-bit systems A and C, 10-bit system B. Three of them timed.
  * The stored form only; lag 0; libaom 3.15.1 at speed 6.
  * Not covered: a second vendor, iterative reconstruction, US cine, a phone, a calibrated display, a reader.
* **Owner's call** (the page's §2, items 1–10):
  * recommended yes: A, the label "lossy AV1 · about N:1", the exact frame fetched without a request (as a
    direction), item 0.2, and dropping "also for diagnosis" (0.9);
  * recommended no: items 0.1, 0.3 and 0.4, option D, and the model observer for now.
  * A yes to A changes the glossary's "meant to be visually lossless".
* **Source.**
  * `claude/reviews-2026-11-28:docs/decisions/visually-lossless.md @ 61611240e`
  * `claude/vl-criterion:docs/av1/vl-criterion.md` §1.4, §3.1 and §5.1 `@ 4accfdb3c`

### 2.2 The noise rule that picks Q reads its bar on a plateau, and at its pick it removes the noise

**Plain.** The rule sets the view's quality from a noise ratio, but the ratio flattens out right where the threshold
sits. So the rule cannot reliably pick one quality step. And at the step it picks, it removes the image noise along
with any faint detail under it.

* **Mechanism.**
  * The ratio r tends to 1 once the noise is deleted.
  * At the rule's step (about 4.8σ), the error is mostly the removed noise: k² ÷ r² is 76–92 % (theory).
* **Numbers.**
  * Between x = 4 and 6, r moves 0.065 while ν moves 0.54 (theory).
  * The measured noise point x* (MEASURE-1 B, 352 cells):
    * 4.7584 at G 1, 4.6359 pooled, 4.8024 on 12-bit volumes;
    * the anchor is unresolved on 15 of 19 volumes, with δ at 0.1–5.5 %.
  * How well each statistic resolves one Q (measured, R1): ν on 15 of 19 volumes, r on 4 of 19.
  * What a faint finding keeps of its detectability (theory, a quantizer model, not AV1):
    * 0.86 at x = 2 (0.96 under 1σ);
    * 0.35 at 4.8σ;
    * 0.20–0.30 when the model is fitted to the noise kept.
  * What the rule may claim (measured, R2, held): it "removes 0.78–0.93 of the fine-band noise's amplitude and adds
    0.28–0.45 σ", for the tiles' mix.
  * The rule's Q costs 0.10–0.28 of HTJ2K's bytes (corrected from Q 8's 0.07–0.22, see §9).
* **How found.** Rows MEASURE-1 B and A, AV1-CORRECT and VL-READS (R1, R2).
* **Limits.**
  * As in 2.1.
  * Two of the five DBT systems in a national QC protocol reconstruct iteratively (source, NHSBSP 1407). On those, a
    criterion relative to the noise has nothing to stand on.
* **Owner's call.** The bar at 1.00 or 1.03, and how to state the anchor: a range [Q_lo, Q_hi] with ν beside it, or
  "keep at least half" (Q_ν).
* **Source.**
  * `claude/audit-measure:docs/av1/audit-protocols.md @ e5f8fff3`
  * `claude/av1-corrections:docs/av1/corrections.md @ 664a6179`

### 2.3 What a quality bound that can be derived would cost: 3–8× today's view

**Plain.** A quality bound the project could actually derive costs three to eight times more bytes than today's view.
Even then it is a bound on a model, not on AV1.

* **Mechanism.** Wherever x ≤ 2 the coding error is white noise (R3, held), so options C and D stand as derived bounds.
* **Numbers.**
  * Option D (x ≤ 2):
    * 0.26–0.59 of HTJ2K's bytes (measured, R8);
    * predicted 0.22–0.55, refuted on `dbts_b2` at 0.594;
    * its label would say no finding loses more than 14 % of its detectability (model).
  * Option C (x ≤ 1): 0.48–0.77 (measured); "under 4 %" lost (model).
  * Option F (ν ≥ ½): 1.16–1.92× the rule's bytes (measured).
  * R3 (measured, 175 cells): w_N 0.99–1.05 and w₄ 0.93–1.08; w_N 0.77–0.87 at the rule's Q on 19 of 19.
* **Limits.**
  * R8's Q is the highest rung that was read, not the highest Q with x ≤ 2. On four volumes the pick is Q 2 with Q 3
    unread.
  * AV1's own detectability kept is unmeasured.
* **Source.** `vl-criterion.md` §3 and §5.1 `@ 4accfdb3c`.

### 2.4 An 8-bit view costs 14–86 % more bytes, and painting today's view at 8 bits fails the noise rule everywhere

**Plain.** A view built for an 8-bit hardware path needs 1.14–1.86 times today's bytes to stay within the noise bar.
Simply rounding today's picture to 8 bits on the way to the screen breaks the bar on all 19 volumes. Fixing that costs
the same extra bytes, so the unchecked shortcut saves nothing. This reopens the 2026-11-10 "no 8-bit preview"
decision.

* **Mechanism.**
  * Cutting to 8 bits adds rounding error under the noise.
  * Rounding error adds to coding error in quadrature, so the coded view then needs a lower Q.
* **Numbers.**
  * Rounding RMS alone (theory): 4.61 source units at D 12 and 1.12 at D 10, which is 0.31–0.44 and 0.20–0.33 of
    σ_min.
  * The 8-bit view (measured):
    * 1.14–1.86× today's bytes at its highest Q under the bar (Q 3–6 at 12 bits, Q 5–11 at 10 bits);
    * the removal model had predicted 0.82–1.09×, which never occurred.
  * 4:2:0 grey adds 0.01–0.12 % of bytes (measured).
  * Rounding today's view after decode (DEPTH8-ROUNDING, measured):
    * the worst frame is at 1.001–1.080 of σ at today's Q, on 19 of 19 volumes;
    * meeting the bar takes 1–3 rungs lower, at 1.17–1.83× today's bytes.
* **How found.** Rows DEPTH8-THEORY, DEPTH8-MEASURE (249 cells over all 19 volumes) and DEPTH8-ROUNDING (59 cells and
  19 round-only rows at G 1). libaom 3.15.1 at cpu 6 on one thread, dav1d 1.5.4.
* **Limits.**
  * One σ estimator.
  * The rounding was modelled in software: no phone's colour conversion, and truncation not measured.
  * G 16 and 4:2:0 were tested on six volumes only.
  * The cores were saturated, so no time is claimed.
  * No browser, hardware decoder, reader or US.
* **Owner's call.** Whether an 8-bit view at 1.14–1.86× the bytes is ever acceptable (PR 389).
* **Source.**
  * `claude/depth8 @ c79d0b07` (theory) and `ddd3ec0e` (rows)
  * `claude/depth8:docs/av1/depth-cut-8.md` §10.2 `@ 853ed8fc`

### 2.5 The exact frame as three strips (Layout 1): about 8 % sooner; kept proposed, not built

**Plain.** Storing each large exact frame as three separately decodable strips cut the Lossless wait by about 8 % on
the one deciding test link. That is enough to keep the format proposed, but not enough to build it before a phone shows
the gain.

* **Mechanism.**
  * Each strip is its own codestream, so three decoders work at once on the same bytes.
  * The decode alone falls by more than half. On a link-bound cell, though, the decode is a small part of the wait.
* **Numbers** (measured, `dbts_b1`, the r5000 link, 4× throttle):
  * Arm medians: D 4 033 ms, its A/A copy D′ 4 025 ms, D+T1 3 945 ms, D+T3 3 724 ms.
  * D+T3 ÷ D is ×0.923.
  * The strips' own share, D+T3 ÷ D+T1, is ×0.943 (s 0.014, n 4).
    * The prediction was ×0.96–0.99, a miss.
    * It is inside the rule's bound of ×0.963, so Layout 1 stays proposed.
  * The lab page's path, D+T1 ÷ D, is ×0.979: about a quarter of the gain.
  * Decode alone on three workers: ×0.43–0.48 at 4×.
  * Bytes: +0.11–0.14 %.
* **How found.** Rows HTJ2K-A1-RUN (288 visits, 234 kept, 6 rounds, 4×4 Williams order) and HTJ2K-A1-REVIEW (all 36
  medians reproduce), plus stage 3 of HTJ2K-MEASURE.
* **Limits.**
  * One link-bound cell of one volume, from 4–5 paired rounds, decides it.
  * The 4× cgroup cap stands in for a phone, which is an assumption.
  * r20000 is decode-bound and decides nothing.
  * No HTJ2K frame has been timed on a phone.
  * Whether strips pay for smaller frames (systems A and C, US) is not measured.
  * UNIFIED-NAMES found that the stage-3 page calls `requestExactFrame`, which its `consumer.js` lacks, yet arm D's
    rows show Lossless answered. That provenance is unexplained.
* **Owner's call** (recommended):
  * Layout 1 carried by D+T3: yes.
  * Build it now: no. A build means a store format, a re-encode at the PACS, a strip binding and a label state per
    strip, and it widens "over 12 bits" in `target.md` to 10-bit DBT.
  * Queue P1 and P2 on a phone: yes.
  * P3, until the cache is on: no.
* **Source.**
  * `claude/reviews-2026-11-28:docs/decisions/exact-frame-layout.md @ 67e5e5671`
  * `claude/htj2k-a1:docs/htj2k-tiles.md` §A1 `@ 4189ef47a`

### 2.6 A smaller exact picture first (D+L★): pixels about 4× sooner, but it is not the original

**Plain.** Showing a reduced-resolution exact picture first puts pixels on screen about four times sooner. But that

* **Mechanism.**

  * It is exact against the codestream's low band, not against the original resampled to the screen.
* **Numbers.**

* **How found.** HTJ2K-MEASURE stage 3, and HTJ2K-PREVIEW-MOBILE with its review and fix (PR 397).
* **Limits.**
  * The quality cell covers `dbts_b1` and `dbts_c1` only.

## 3. Decode and devices

### 3.1 No browser path hands back a hardware-decoded frame at full precision; only a native app could

**Plain.** No browser today, on a phone or a desktop, lets the viewer read back a hardware-decoded frame at full
precision and check it. Only an app outside the browser can. This makes "fast hardware decode" and "every pixel
checked" a choice between the two in the browser, unless the view drops to 8 bits (§2.4).

* **Mechanism.**
  * Android wraps a MediaCodec frame as ABGR, and `copyTo` gives BGRX.
  * Desktop Chromium has no P010 format and refuses 4:0:0 at gate 2.
  * WebGPU and WebGL go through 8-bit images.
  * Only two routes return samples that an XXH3 can check:
    * desktop Chromium's 8-bit GREY420 (an NV12 plane copy);
    * native clients, which get `P010` planes.
* **Numbers.**
  * None measured.
  * `copyTo({format: "I420P10"})` is a NotSupportedError by spec and in Chromium 157/158; in Chromium 141 it crashes
    the renderer (source).
  * The review re-fetched 15 randomly drawn quoted claims, and all 15 held.
* **How found.** Rows HW-DECODE-DEEP, HW10-ROUTES and their reviews, and MOBILE-OPT-OTHER-CODECS. Read in the Chromium
  157 and 158 sources, Dawn, Vulkan-Docs and the codec specs.
* **Limits.**
  * Sources only; no device, no GPU.
  * The same holds for H.264, H.265 and VP9 above 8 bits.
* **The probe page** (rows HW-PROBE-PAGE and its review, PR 393; Chrome for Testing 153 on SwiftShader):
  * It works and records every outcome: 11 claims pass, paint skipped (measured, headless).
  * The review found (measured, cause not traced) that every RGB `copyTo` and every WebGL2 read fits a studio-range
    expansion, even when the frame says `fullRange: true`.
  * So the probe's RGB and paint "exact" counts measure range, not precision. `ingest.py`'s premise that "full range
    gives R = G = B" fails in this Chromium.
* **Source.**
  * `claude/hw-decode:docs/decode/hardware.md @ c5f830b2`, `b631179a`
  * `claude/unified:docs/decode/hardware-10bit.md @ bbceb9e35`
  * `claude/hw-probe @ 0d330405`, `af4b6aeb`

### 3.2 The native app stays paused, recorded as the only 10-bit hardware route

**Plain.** A native phone app is the only way a hardware decoder can hand over 10-bit pixels that can be checked. But
no class of Android phone is promised a hardware decoder that takes a whole DBT frame. So native stays paused, and
resumes only when a phone's codec list shows such a decoder.

* **Mechanism.** A hardware decoder helps only if it accepts the frame's size and the AV1 level that size needs.
* **Numbers.**
  * 8 of the 19 DBT volumes need AV1 level 5.x, and 11 fit 4.x (derived from their sizes).
  * 17 of the 19 exceed portrait 1080p (derived).
  * Android class V promises Main 10 at level 4.1, "only required to support 1080p" (source, CDD 16 §2.2.7.1).
  * 2025 phone shipments: entry (< $200) 40.6 % (source), middle 38.3 % (derived), premium (≥ $800) 21.1 % (source).
  * The software view's effective time a frame with three decoders (theory): entry 90–200 ms, middle 41–132 ms, premium
    33–48 ms. The round's phone measured 133–135 ms a frame, which is 45 ms effective.
  * On desktop, native buys nothing on a clean link (measured, M2).
    * Under 2 % loss at 20 Mbit/s it fills ~4 % faster, and shows the first frame 161–206 ms sooner.
    * That was on lavapipe with no GPU; the GPU desktop run with Vulkan Video never ran.
* **How found.** Rows DEVICE-THEORY (PR 414), AV1-HW-CHIPS (PR 397) and HW10-ROUTES (PR 392), and the decision page
  PAUSED-LINES-PAGE.
* **Limits.**
  * One phone.
  * One benchmark's single-thread rating stands in for decoder speed.
  * Shipment price bands stand in for readers' phones.
  * No phone has yet reported whether it lists AV1 Main 10.
* **Owner's call** (recommended yes):
  * native stays paused through phase 2;
  * native recorded as the 10-bit hardware route, conditional on a device;
  * one cloud row moves native's lab settings out of `window.rs` and `count.rs` before `main`.
* **Source.**
  * `claude/reviews-2026-11-28:docs/decisions/paused-lines.md @ a5c129f4d`

### 3.3 On a phone, software AV1 stays the route of record; the other options, ranked

**Plain.** Software AV1 is the only route ever timed on a phone. Hardware decode can be checked only at 8 bits, and
then costs more bytes. So software AV1 stays the default until a phone round reports. The survey's labelled lean was
to crop the border first (since measured, §2.11), then lossy HTJ2K or the "inverted" hardware frame.

* **The options.**
  * **Software AV1.**
    * 133–135 ms a `dbts_b1-q8-g8` frame with three decoders, ~22 frames/s (measured: the only phone number).
    * Chromium's `optimizeForLatency` sets dav1d's frame delay to 1, and each decoder gets min(15, cores) threads
      (source).
    * 2×2 tiles: ×0.64–0.72 on the cloud host (§2.8).
  * **The inverted hardware frame.** A studio-swing grey frame, inverted per frame so it can be checked.
    * At most 7.78 bits, 220 levels (derived).
    * Cells on 19 volumes (measured, saturated host): it costs 1.16–2.49× today's bytes. I4 missed, because luma
      leaves 16…235 and leaves ~200 levels.
    * It reads back and hashes 8.5 MB a `dbts_b1` frame, half of today's (derived).
  * **Unchecked 8-bit hardware.**
    * Each step is 2.0–7.4 display levels, against 0.5–1.85 today.
    * e/σ is 1.02–1.09 on screen, above the bar (derived).
  * **GPU compute decode of HTJ2K** (only the 5/3 transform is exact).
    * 0.621 ns a sample, 5.3 ms a frame (theory, from an ICIP 2019 figure).
    * It breaks even if the phone's GPU is no more than 25× slower.
    * Built in the lab: 1 158 of 1 158 frames exact on SwiftShader.
  * **Software HTJ2K.**
    * 78–127 ms a `dbts_b1` frame on a phone (theory).
    * The cloud measures 6.7–9.7 ns a sample.
    * Bytes are 4.6–14.6× the view at Q 8.
* **How found.** Rows MOBILE-DECODE-OPTIONS, the six MOBILE-OPT-* advocates, and MOBILE-DECODE-COMPARE with its review
  (2 blockers) and fix. Asks M4–M22 were added for the phone round.
* **Limits.**
  * Nothing was run on a phone or a GPU.
  * Three DBT volumes were timed, 19 for bytes.
  * Whether the round's phone has an AV1 hardware decoder at all is unsettled.
  * The HEVC "> 95 %" figure is unverified.
  * The shipped OpenJPH 0.31.0 predates upstream's CVE fix; the bump is in 3.9.
* **Owner's calls.**
  * `design.md` §Decided records "hardware painting first, with the check after, is acceptable but not ideal".
  * A decode at the screen's size would reopen "no low-resolution preview".
* **Source.**
  * `claude/mobile-decode:docs/decode/mobile/README.md @ ab0dddf0`, corrected `@ 84dbdef2`
  * the option files at `e1cbe8fe`, `7949590c`, `df1e4c12`, `65940452`, `a9422dee` and `f4021c40`

### 3.4 The phone prediction was written down before the round

**Plain.** Before measuring the phone, a model predicted its decode speed. It also predicted that on the high-detail
volume, sending the exact series alone (arm B) would not show up as faster than the view, unless the link is very
fast.

* **Mechanism.** Three inputs:
  * the fast cores' published single-thread rating, over the cloud reference's;
  * the number of fast cores;
  * the platform's storage and decoder floors.

  A fill is taken as max(wire, floor) + the rest.
* **Numbers** (all theory):

### 8.3 What the green mark proves, and what it cannot see

**Plain.** The frame check catches damage anywhere up to the decoder's output. It cannot see damage to the settings
that turn samples into a picture, or anything after the decoder. So every frame should carry a checksum, and each
series' settings file should be checksummed too.

* **Mechanism.**
  * The check covers the store, the server, the wire, the receive code and the decoder.
  * It cannot cover the held frame, the texture, the window parameters, the index or the label.
  * The window table, the slope and the depth cut's shift all come from `metadata.json`, which the check never sees.
    A damaged window or slope paints correct pixels wrongly, under a green mark.
* **Numbers.**
  * The check costs 0.15 ms on 512², 2.8 ms at 1914 × 2572 and 8 ms at 3328 × 4096, in both engines (measured, x86).
  * On a phone it would be 1–10 % of a decode (theory).
  * One hash of a few kilobytes per series would cover `metadata.json` (theory, not built).
  * Damage at rest: 0.86 % of nearline and 0.065 % of enterprise disks developed a checksum mismatch within 41 months
    (source, FAST'08).
  * Predicted, not run: four faults pass every check under a green label:
    * a damaged `metadata.json` display parameter;
    * a held frame damaged after its check;
    * a stale texture;
    * frame j painted for frame i.
* **Limits.**
  * No phone, no hardware decoder, no WebKit, no US cine, no radio.
  * A phone's memory fault rate is published nowhere.
* **Source.** `frame-integrity.md` §3.3 and §4 `@ 070e19ee8`; `claude/unified:docs/adr/exactness-in-production.md`
  `@ af3d135e3`.

### 8.4 Checking every view frame against the encoder's own picture: cheap in the browser, costly in the ingest as built (PR 368)

**Plain.** A fingerprint of the picture the encoder made lets the viewer prove it decoded exactly that, for about 2–4 %
of load time. Until it ships, the green "Optimal quality" label claims a check that nobody ran. Computing the
fingerprint and the coding error together in the ingest, as built, adds about half again to the ingest's time,
five times its bar.

* **Mechanism.**
  * AV1 without film grain has one right output (source, AV1 §7.2), so an XXH3 of the ingest's dav1d decode can be
    checked in any browser.
  * A mismatched inter frame's group is decoded again on dav1d.
* **Numbers in the browser** (measured, 504 interleaved fills over 18 series):
  * Fill: +3.7 % at 1× (−3.5 to +7.5 %) and +1.9 % at 4×. The adoption rule held.
  * First picture: +7.8 % median at 1× (to +12.9 %) and +3.5 % at 4×. Recorded, not weighed.
  * The hash is ~3.6–4.2 % of one decode (derived).
  * 41 of 45 mutants killed.
* **Numbers in the ingest** (measured, I2-RUN, 12 cells, 3 interleaved rounds):
  * 461–462 s off, 683–740 s on: +48 to +60 %, against a 10 % bar.

  * The noise-band ratio is over the study's by +27–30 % (a1), +69–71 % (c1) and +121–122 % (b1), against a 1 % bar.

  * A mutant survives: σ without its rounding term, at σ > ~2.
* **Limits.**
  * No hardware AV1 decoder in any cell, and that is the case the digest exists for.
  * One host, `--cpu-used=6`.

  * PR 368 cannot be merged on GitHub (an old base).

    refused frame throws.
* **Owner's call.**

  * Either take the study's in-window mask, or keep the whole-frame ratio labelled not comparable.
  * Either accept +50 % ingest time, or compute the ratio off the ingest's path. The integrity page recommends off the
    path, with the mask.
