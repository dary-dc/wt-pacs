CONCLUSION -- the prediction in S5-CONTROL-ARM.md is DISPROVED, for a structural reason.

That document says: "This host has 4 vCPU, where the loop term is only a tie. On 8 CPU the
loop resolves at -33.6%, so the lazy arm's hit-regime advantage should be LARGER there, not
smaller -- that is the one cell still to run."

Ran. It is SMALLER: hybrid_lazyring - hybrid on hits is -3.2% / -2.9% here against
-10.9% / -9.0% on the 4-vCPU sandbox. Both are ties, so neither is a claim under the rule,
but the direction of the prediction is wrong and the reason is not noise.

hybrid_lazyring and hybrid share the ring-shaped loop AND the miss mechanism. The only
thing that differs between them is whether a ring is constructed when no read misses.
So that delta is governed by the idle-ring term alone -- R in the hit regime -- and NOT
by the loop term L. L is already inside both arms; it cannot widen the gap between them.

And the idle ring is CHEAPER on this host, not dearer:
  R on hits    this host (8 cpu)  +5.4% / +4.6%      sandbox (4 cpu)  +17.5% / +14.2%
There is simply less idle ring to save here, so the lazy arm saves less. The prediction
conflated two different terms: "L resolves on 8 CPU" is true (v25) and irrelevant to what
hybrid_lazyring - hybrid measures.

What does hold: deferring construction still costs nothing where the ring IS needed --
mix -1.2% / +0.7% and miss +0.2% / +0.0%, ties with medians at or below one percent, and
tighter than the sandbox's (+1.9/+3.4% on miss). The design is still free. It just buys
less on this host than on the smaller one.

Against pool, Z tracks L+R closely and beats it slightly on hits:

  mix    Z -46.4 / -50.6%    L+R -43.5 / -49.8%   (both RESOLVED)

Two things about this run that argue against reading it too hard:

1. L on hits did NOT reproduce v25's RESOLVED. Here it is -26.5% / -25.4%, a tie, against
   v25's -33.6% / -32.9% RESOLVED on the SAME host with the SAME hit-cell population
   (n=113/114 in both; C_readers contributes nothing to the hit bucket, only 24 cells to
   mix and 12 to miss). That is ~7pp of campaign-to-campaign drift straddling the 28.5%
   threshold. L on hits is at the edge on this host, not comfortably past it -- and the
   headline of v25 should be read with that in mind.

2. The mix rows rest on 13 and 12 cells, against v25's 37 and 36, because this cell drops
   C_readers. n=12 is barely above the script's MIN_N of 5. L's mix row RESOLVED here
   (-48.6 / -50.1%) where v25 called it a flat tie (-2.6 / -2.5%); on a bucket that small,
   over a population that changed, that is not a contradiction worth carrying yet.

Caveats: same physical machine as v23/v25 (btrfs-on-LUKS, compress=zstd:1, stock 128 KiB
read-ahead) -- a second core count for the lazy-ring question, not a new filesystem.
Host was not idle; load average 1.35 at start. Arms interleave within each repeat (`pos`),
and the two-run reproduction is the guard.
