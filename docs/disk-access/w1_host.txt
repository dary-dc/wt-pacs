Chosen: 16 KiB -> --stride 250000 (STEP 16 frames);  250 kB -> --stride 500000.

Why 250000 and not 65536 at 16 KiB, when the rule says "smallest >= 0.95": 65536 reads
96.1 %, four times the hit contamination of the sandbox cells this run is compared against,
and it is not on the plateau. 250000 clears the floor at 98.4-99.2 %, is the stride the
sandbox column used (x18_host.txt), and is what read_path_ab.sh hardcodes and server_ab.sh's
default STEP resolves to. Diverging would have made the two columns incomparable, which is
the whole point of the run. The floor was not lowered; a stride above the minimum was taken.
At 250 kB the rule and the plateau agree, so 500000 is both.

Fixture deviation, required on this host: btrfs is mounted compress=zstd:1 and
gen_live_cell_fixture.sh writes a constant byte (0xAB), which compresses ~32:1 while du and
stat report full size. Every fixture here was generated after
`btrfs property set <dir> compression none`, verified 0 "encoded" extents in filefrag -v
(84 MB -> 1 extent, 512 MB -> 1, 1 GiB -> 4). Same trap and same fix as v34_scale_host.txt.

250 kB depth 16 is PAST saturation: x1.09 for a 4x depth increase, and all five arms land
within 5.3 % of each other (6046-6366 asks/s). Every 250 kB depth-16 comparison in
w1_250k_arms.tsv is therefore a tie by construction and no arm claim may be made there.
16 KiB depth 16 is NOT saturated: x2.27 still climbing, arms spread 10.9 %.

Ceiling seen here is ~1.5-1.6 GB/s, against v34's ~840 MB/s on the same machine. The two do
not conflict, they are different access patterns: v34 walked an 8 GB fixture at an 8 MiB
stride (scattered), this run walks a 512 MB fixture at a 500 kB stride (semi-sequential, two
frames apart). Treat 1.5-1.6 GB/s as this host's best case and 840 MB/s as its scattered case.
CAVEAT: a 512 MB fixture can sit inside a consumer NVMe's own DRAM/SLC cache, which page-cache
residency (mincore, 99.6 % miss here) cannot see. The 250 kB bandwidth figures are an upper
bound for that reason; v34's 8 GB fixture is the safer number for a device claim.
Warm figures (4.0-5.0 GB/s at 16 KiB, 5.8-8.3 GB/s at 250 kB) are the page-cache ceiling and
are not a device measurement at all.

--- one instrument change, disclosed ---
lab/scripts/server_ab.sh: the poll for a session's `session reads ...` log line was 30 x 0.1 s
(3 s) and aborted the run at fill_r5 with "the cold control is unreadable". A fill session
reads past what the driver consumes (hits=278..319 for 256 asks) and only ends when a write
finally fails, which took longer than 3 s; the line arrived only when the abort killed the
server. Raised to 200 x 0.1 s (20 s). The guard still hard-fails if the line never comes --
no measured quantity is affected, and no threshold was relaxed.
