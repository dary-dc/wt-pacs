Mechanism -- L by depth, hit regime, readers=1 (the core-count question S5 left open):

           depth      1      2      4      8     16     32     64
  8 cpu (this host)  -0.6  -11.4  -27.5  -42.2  -44.2  -45.7  -35.9
  4 cpu (v24)        -8.6  -11.2  -23.1  -21.6  -18.0  -12.7   -4.4

  Sign agreement on this host's resolved depths: 36/36, 35/35, 36/36, 24/24, 23/24.

  reader_pool spawns `depth` Tokio tasks; reader_ring and pool_ringloop hold `depth`
  slots in ONE task. On 4 vCPUs the extra tasks stop running concurrently once depth
  passes the core count, so the per-task cost is masked by the queueing that would have
  happened anyway -- L peaks at depth 4 and decays to nothing by 64. On 8 CPUs the
  task-per-slot loop keeps paying scheduler and per-task heap cost against real
  parallelism, so L grows to -45.7% at depth 32 and is still -35.9% at 64.

  This is what S5 predicted -- "core count is precisely where a task-per-slot loop would
  be expected to differ" -- and it is why one host was not enough to call the loop a tie.
