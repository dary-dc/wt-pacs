## 2 · What was measured, and the three estimator lessons

Numbers are in §10 and the window ADR; do not re-derive. Three findings a new session would
otherwise walk into again:

- **A first-send-to-last-receive throughput is biased.** It folds one round trip into eight
  intervals and stalled the window's climb at 4 where the formula says 8. `Tf` is the median
  time between arrivals.
- **Ask-to-receive time is not an RTT** once asks queue: it reads `RTT + D·Tf` and an estimator
  fed with it climbs to the clamp. RTT is `getStats().smoothedRtt`, or the smallest of at least
  two asks sent into an idle window.
- **A session's first ask carries its warm-up.** In headless Chromium 141 that single idle
  sample drove the depth to 16 until the two-sample rule was added; now the same cell holds.
- Headless Chromium 141 here has **no `getStats`** on WebTransport and `congestionControl` is
  null; the owner's browser rig runs 148, unverified. Its nested timers clamp to ≈4 ms.
- The 28 ms depth-1 tail on loopback is 25 ms of `max_ack_delay` against a 0.1 ms RTT; priced
  for the target it is under a millisecond per frame at 1 % loss (NEXT item 7).

## 6 · Pitfalls of this environment, learned the hard way

- A Bash chain ending in `&` backgrounds its own variable assignments; write a script file.
- `pkill -f '<pattern>'` kills the shell whose command line contains the pattern; kill by pid
  from `pgrep` with `$$` excluded.
- `git checkout <file>` restores the **index**, not the last edit; keep a scratch copy before a
  mutation and restore from it.
- The gate rebuilds bundles from the tree at the moment it runs; do not edit while it runs, or
  run it again after.
- Run `cargo test -p exact-server --quiet <name>` to see the lib test's own summary line; the bin
  target prints a 0-test summary last.
- Foreground `sleep` is refused; a foreground `cargo` command waits on the build lock and is
  the natural way to wait for a background build.
