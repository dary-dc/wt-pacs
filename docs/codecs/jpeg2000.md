# JPEG 2000 Part 1

Measured for contrast, never served. The side-by-side table is [`README.md`](README.md); HTJ2K, its Part 15
successor, is [`htj2k.md`](htj2k.md).

## What it is

ISO/IEC 15444-1 | ITU-T T.800: a wavelet codec whose reversible 5/3 path is lossless, its code-blocks coded by
EBCOT, with quality layers that make one codestream lossy first and exact at its end. HTJ2K keeps its wavelet and
codestream and replaces EBCOT.

## How we use it (or would)

Lab only: row EMBED coded every series as Part 1 with three lossy quality layers then lossless (OpenJPEG 2.5.4), to
see whether one intra codestream can be its own preview. Not proposed for the product.

## Bytes

**Part 1 is 4–7 % smaller than HTJ2K** on row EMBED's seven sets, and the layers cost 0.09–0.19 % over a single
layer. WG-04's slides put the gap at about 5 % ([`../av1/lossless-literature.md`](../av1/lossless-literature.md) §1).

## Decode speed

**The exact frame decodes 6–12× slower than OpenJPH** in WASM (OpenJPEG), slower in 210/210 paired rounds: EBCOT,
the block coder HTJ2K replaces, costs what dav1d-WASM does. The first layer decodes in 1.0–1.4× OpenJPH's time for
the exact frame. Headless Chromium 141, this container, 15 interleaved rounds at 1× and 4×; 22 680/22 680 frames
matched ([`lab/av1/bytes/embedded`](../../lab/av1/bytes/embedded/README.md)).

## Total time

Unmeasured.

## Client resources

Code: OpenJPEG's WASM decoder, sized in [`lab/av1/bytes/embedded`](../../lab/av1/bytes/embedded/README.md). Memory:
unmeasured.

## Browser and device support

No browser decodes it natively today (Safari dropped it in 18: [`../av1/split-prior-art.md`](../av1/split-prior-art.md)
§2); OpenJPEG in WASM was exact in headless Chromium 141 (row EMBED). Other engines: not run.

## Bit depths, signed, colour

To 38 bits in the standard ([`../av1/split-prior-art.md`](../av1/split-prior-art.md) §2), signed through the SIZ
marker as HTJ2K is ([`../decode/README.md`](../decode/README.md) §Signed), RGB through the reversible colour
transform. Exact here on every frame of row EMBED's seven sets.

## Random access and on-demand

Every frame is its own codestream; an ask decodes one frame.

## Progressive / preview

**Quality layers make the codestream its own preview** (OpenJPEG 2.5.4, reversible 5/3, LRCP, three lossy layers
then lossless): the layered codestream is **0.93–0.96 of HTJ2K's served bytes, preview included**. Its first layer
is **0.4–0.9 % of them at 37–43 dB** on grey (25 dB on the RGB ultrasound, provisional: a lossy-sourced set), and the
third is 7–15 % at 44–59 dB. For the same PSNR the layers take about twice AV1's preview bytes (fluoroscopy 44 dB:
1.7 % against 0.78 %). Those bytes are inside the exact frame; AV1's come on top of it
([`../av1/README.md`](../av1/README.md) §Preview).

## Exactness risks and how they're checked

Each frame against the checksum written from the encoder's input, as every codec here. OpenJPEG is also this
project's independent witness for HTJ2K: for signed samples ([`../decode/README.md`](../decode/README.md) §Signed)
and for a reduced resolution level, which it clamps to the sample range ([`../decode/README.md`](../decode/README.md) §A frame at the level the screen
needs).

## DICOM standing

In the standard since Supplement 61 (2001): `1.2.840.10008.1.2.4.90` lossless only and `.91` (PS3.6 2026d, PS3.5
§8.2.4).

## Maturity / tooling

OpenJPEG 2.5.4, pinned in the lab; its encode time was not measured.

## Where it wins

The fewest bytes of the JPEG 2000 family, and a small preview inside the exact bytes at no cost on the wire.

## Where it loses

Decode: every exact frame several times slower than HTJ2K's.
